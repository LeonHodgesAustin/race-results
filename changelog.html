What's new

All notable changes to racetelemetry are recorded here, newest first. This file is written for anyone browsing a published dashboard — not just developers — so entries describe what changed for you as a viewer or driver, in plain language.

Unreleased

Not released yet

Added

  • Ghost car. Session and track pages now list the simulated lap as "Ghost car". Tick it like any lap to race it in replay, overlay its speed and see the time gap against your lap, corner by corner. It drives your own line at the limits your laps showed, and it never counts as a best lap.
  • A quick lap-chart page for every session. Opening a session now lands on Overview: the best lap as a timing-clock readout, every lap's sector splits and gap to the best, and a glance at the fastest lap's line and speed. The full map, lap comparison charts, corner stats and gate editor are still there, one click away on Analysis — nothing was removed, it's just split across two pages instead of one long one.
  • A "Sensors on" button for the live tools. With recording stopped, the status page and the Live tools page can now turn the sensors on without recording anything, so the live tools have something to show. It switches itself off after 30 minutes, Start recording takes over from it, and it's never offered while a recording is running.
  • One bookmark for the logger's pages. The logger's address changes whenever the phone's hotspot restarts, so a bookmark to it stopped working. The Base Station now keeps http://127.0.0.1:8090/logger/ (and /logger/live.html) pointing at wherever the logger is, so you can bookmark that once on the phone.

Changed

  • A new look for the dashboard and published site. The rounded cards and blue accent are gone, replaced by a trackside timing-ledger look: hairline rules instead of card shadows, a teal accent, and the same purple/green/ amber meanings (best of any lap / best of your selection / slower) carried over. Nothing about what the dashboard shows changed, only how it looks.
  • Gearing shown as speed per 100 RPM. The live RPM panel now shows how many mph the car is doing per 100 RPM right now, next to what each gear should give (speed = that number x RPM / 100), instead of RPM per mph. A clutch slipping or a gear mismatch shows up as the two not matching.

1.22.0

27 Oct 2026Latest

Added

  • Live tools on the logger's own web page. A new Live page shows, as it happens: engine RPM and gear from the vibration sensor, a force circle with both motion sensors drawn together (their dots blend where they agree and fade over a few seconds), a scrolling vibration spectrogram, and a short trace of where the car has been with the GPS error drawn as circles. Each tool switches on and off; with them all off the logger sends nothing extra.
  • The logger's status and settings pages were rebuilt as ordinary web pages talking to the logger, with no change to what they show. The confirmation before Stop, Shut down and the other controls is now a pop-up on the page; the logger still refuses them while the car is moving.

Fixed

  • Summit Point is in the right place. The example config put Summit Point's start/finish about 16 miles from the track, so the logger couldn't recognise it and would have recorded sessions there without lap times. It now has the real start/finish line.
  • The Base Station finds the logger more reliably. Looking the logger up by name could return a garbled address when the phone also heard its IPv6 address, which then got remembered as the logger's address.
  • Sessions get the right time even when the logger boots without it. The logger has no clock of its own while it's off; if it booted with no internet and before GPS had a fix, a whole session could be stamped with the wrong time (28 minutes off on 2026-10-24). It now sets its time from the GPS at the first fix, before the session starts.

1.21.0

26 Oct 2026

Added

  • A demo of the engine views. The home page has a new "Demo: engine RPM and gear" card. It opens a dashboard of real laps from Watkins Glen with the engine simulated from their speed: revs through every lap, the gear, shift points, time over the limit and the whole session's revs against the clock -- what the dashboard will show once the car records its engine on track. A banner on the page says what's real and what's made up.

1.20.0

24 Oct 2026

Added

  • Engine RPM from vibration, with no wiring to the engine. The new racetelemetry rpm command reads engine speed from the logger's vibration sensor and writes it out over time, with an optional chart of RPM against road speed. On its first real run it read a blip of the throttle at 7,100 RPM; the tach's high-water mark showed about 7,000. It reads nothing while the engine is off.
  • Support for a second, dedicated vibration sensor. The logger can now read an ICM-42688-P accelerometer 1,000 times a second, alongside the existing motion sensor, for working out engine RPM across the whole rev range. It's off unless a [vib] section is set in the logger's config, and adds roughly 40 MB per hour of recording. The status page shows it is live and whether any readings were lost.
  • Stop recording, shut down and reboot from the status page. A new Controls card on the logger's status page has Stop / Start recording, Restart recording (to apply a settings change), Shut down and Reboot, so the end of a session needs no command line. Each asks "are you sure?" first, and all of them are refused while the car is moving. After a stop, the page shows when the session file closed and when it's safe to switch the logger off.
  • The logger now records engine vibration, 500 times a second. A new high-rate channel from the motion sensor is the raw material for working out engine RPM and gear changes from vibration later, with no wiring to the engine. It's off unless raw_accel_rate_hz is set in the logger's config (500 on ours), and adds roughly 250 MB per hour of recording.
  • Every motion-sensor reading is kept, with no gaps when the logger is busy. Each sensor now runs on its own, so a slow save to the memory card, or one sensor hiccuping, no longer interrupts the others. On a road drive that used to cost up to 1.8 seconds of GPS and motion data at a time. Each reading also carries the sensor's own sequence number, so any that were lost can be counted exactly, and each GPS fix keeps the receiver's own clock time.
  • A sensor that won't start no longer stops the recording. If the motion sensor is unplugged, say, the logger keeps recording GPS and lap times and keeps retrying the sensor in the background.
  • The Logger's status page now shows how hot the Pi got while you were driving. The Pi health panel has a chart of CPU temperature over the past hour, with dashed lines at 70 °C (warm) and 80 °C (where the Pi starts slowing itself down), plus the peak temperature and what time it happened. Nobody watches the page from the cockpit, so this is the way to check afterwards whether the box ran hot out on track.
  • "Throttled since boot" on the status page. Alongside whether the Pi is throttling right now, the page now says whether it has had low voltage, overheating or a slowed-down CPU at any point since it was switched on, even if it had recovered by the time you looked.
  • Speed in gears, from the gearing that was actually on the car. A new gearing command replaces the Speed in Gears spreadsheet: for the gearset, final drive and tires on the car it shows the speed at the shift point and at the rev limit in each gear, the step between gears, and where the revs land after each upshift. A new car.toml records which gearing was on the car when, so a change made between sessions applies from the next session on. Give it a session and it also turns that session's top speed into revs in top gear, to see whether the gearing ran out at the end of the longest straight. The revs after an upshift are now worked out exactly; the spreadsheet showed every drop a few hundred RPM too big.
  • Engine RPM and gear on the dashboard. For a session recorded with the vibration sensor, the lap comparison has a new RPM chart: each lap's revs along the lap, with the gear when you hover, and dotted and dashed lines at the shift point and the rev limit. The stats table gains an Engine section: the highest revs in each lap, time spent over the limit, and how many gear changes. A new Engine chart below the stats shows the whole session's revs and speed against the time of day, laps shaded, so the warm-up, pit lane and cool-down are there too. The gear allows for clutchless shifts (the moment between gears when the revs move on their own), the clutch and wheelspin, and only appears once car.toml says which gearing was on the car. Sessions without the vibration sensor look exactly as before.
  • Engine RPM in show. The whole-session plots for a test drive (no laps needed) add an RPM row under speed when the session has vibration data.

Fixed

  • Stopping the logger now takes about a second, not fifteen. Stopping recording (from the status page, the power button or a shutdown) used to hang until the system force-stopped it 15 seconds later. No data was lost, but the status page's Stop button would sit there, and the power button's shutdown was held up.
  • A clock correction mid-session no longer shifts the recorded data. The logger has no clock of its own and sets it from GPS, so it sometimes gets corrected again while recording, by a few seconds at most. Lap times were already safe from that; now every GPS, motion and temperature reading is too, so the speed and G-force traces stay lined up with each other through the correction instead of jumping. The logger's status page says when it has happened and by how much.
  • Lap times can no longer be thrown off by the logger's clock being corrected mid-session. On an overnight test the logger's clock was nudged forward 3 seconds by an internet time check; inside a lap that would have made the lap 3 seconds slower. Laps are now timed on the GPS receiver's own clock, which never jumps (and is a touch more precise too), and the logger sets its clock from GPS much more exactly when it starts up.
  • At home, the logger starts recording right away instead of waiting for the GPS. It sets its own clock from GPS before recording, because it has no clock of its own to fall back on. Indoors, with no GPS signal, that could hold up recording for up to 17 minutes even when the clock had already been set over the internet. It now skips that wait as soon as the clock is set over the internet, and still uses GPS at the track.

1.19.1

11 Oct 2026

Fixed

  • Every lap now starts and finishes on the start/finish line. On the Smoothed trace (the default), a lap's line used to begin and end anywhere up to a couple of metres either side of the line, so laps visibly started in different places. Each lap's line now arrives exactly on the line, where it was timed, while staying wherever it was across the track.

1.19.0

11 Oct 2026

Added

  • Smoother lines on the map. Laps are now drawn as smooth curves through their recorded points instead of straight lines between them, so a corner no longer shows little kinks where the points are a few metres apart (most noticeable on Raw, and wherever the motion sensor dropped out). A new Curves button next to the trace choices turns it off to see the points joined directly. It only changes how the line is drawn, and where replay and chart-cursor dots sit on it: distances, gaps and times still come from the recorded points. A GPS outage is never curved over; it keeps its dashed line.
  • IMU dropout tag. A lap where the motion sensor (IMU) stopped reporting for half a second or more is tagged "IMU dropout" in the lap list, with how long it was out on hover. G isn't measured across the hole, so the G charts now leave a gap there instead of drawing a straight line through the corner, and the Smoothed trace is coarser there because it falls back to GPS points. Lap and sector times aren't affected.

1.18.1

11 Oct 2026

Fixed

  • Tracks published from another computer look right on the session list. A track whose pages were last published by an older version (from the computer that holds that track's data) now shows as a proper card like the rest, instead of plain unstyled text.

1.18.0

11 Oct 2026

Added

  • The logger now notes when saving data holds everything up. On a road drive on 2026-10-11 both the GPS and the motion sensor went quiet at the same moment, for 1.8 seconds and later 0.6 seconds. The logger now writes a warning to its log whenever saving to its memory card takes longer than 0.2 seconds, and reports the slowest save when a recording ends, so the next drive shows whether that's the cause.

Changed

  • A new look for the whole site. The session list, the What's new page and every dashboard now share one clean, light design, with a dark version for night-time viewing. Pages follow your phone or computer's light/dark setting, and the sun/moon button at the top switches it (your browser remembers the choice).
  • The session list is easier to scan. Each track gets one card with its best lap, the car's potential, and a single "Open track dashboard" button. Sessions are named by day ("Fri 25 Sep · Session 2") and each shows how far its best lap was from the track's best.
  • Dashboard headline numbers are now tiles across the top: best lap, theoretical best, car potential, consistency, and a tile for the laps you've ticked.
  • The lap list sits beside the map on a computer, grouped by session day, with each lap's time and gap to the fastest lap. The fastest lap is tagged "Best", and laps with a GPS dropout are tagged instead of struck through. A lap only gets a colour once you tick it, and the first two laps you compare are always blue and orange, so they're easy to tell apart on the map.
  • The map controls are grouped and labelled (Show, Colour, Trace) instead of one long row of buttons.
  • The charts share one "Lap comparison" card on a computer: switch between Stacked, Speed, Long. G and Gap with the tabs at its top. On a phone each chart is still its own tab at the bottom of the screen.
  • What's new lists every version down the side, so you can jump straight to one; the newest is marked "Latest".
  • Times and gaps are set in a fixed-width font so columns line up, and the fonts come with the site, so pages look the same on the paddock hotspot with no internet.

1.17.0

9 Oct 2026

Added

  • The logger can talk to its IMU over a serial line instead of the I2C bus. The new IMU (the BNO085) doesn't get on well with the Raspberry Pi's I2C bus: it's why its readings have sometimes frozen until the logger reset it, and why the bus had to be slowed right down. Over a serial line neither problem exists, and the link is about 60 times faster, which leaves room to take more readings a second later. It's an option, not a change: the logger keeps using I2C unless you switch it in the settings, and the IMU needs four wires moved to use it (see the wiring guide). Switching back is the same in reverse.

1.16.0

3 Oct 2026

Added

  • Car potential: how much of your lap time is the car and conditions, and how much is the driving. Each session's laps show what the car could do that day: how hard it cornered, braked and accelerated. A simulation then drives a model car round the track at exactly those limits, hills included (from USGS lidar). The result is the lap the car could have done.
    • The dashboard shows it as a Car potential chip.
    • A Car column in the stats table gives the car's time for the lap and for every corner and straight, next to how far your best lap is from it.
    • On the track page, each session's own potential sits beside its heading, so you can see the car and track get quicker (or slower) through a weekend, separately from the driving.

    At Watkins Glen the car's potential across all sessions was 2:07.84. The race winner that weekend ran 2:07.63. The new racetelemetry lapsim command prints the same breakdown in the terminal. How it works, and how far to trust it: docs/lapsim.md.

1.15.0

2 Oct 2026

Changed

  • Watkins Glen's G-force numbers are now an estimate of the real values, marked "reconstructed". The new IMU lost much of the steady cornering force in those sessions (see 1.14.0), so the measured numbers were far too low. Cornering G is now worked out from the line the car actually drove (GPS speed and how fast it was changing direction, sharpened with the gyro). Braking and acceleration combine GPS speed changes with the accelerometer. Peak cornering G on the fastest Glen laps now reads about 1.6 g, and average combined G about 0.75 g (it was about 0.5 g). The G Stats table and the Longitudinal G chart show a "reconstructed" tag on those laps, with an explanation behind the help button. Trust the shape and the comparison between laps more than any single peak value, because a slide can still push a peak a little high. Sessions recorded from 1.14.0 on use the measured values as normal.

1.14.0

2 Oct 2026

Fixed

  • G-force and the smoothed trace now work properly for sessions recorded with the new IMU (the BNO085), including all of Watkins Glen. The dashboards work out how the logger box is mounted in the car before splitting acceleration into cornering, braking and up-down. That relied on feeling gravity, and the new IMU's recordings have gravity already taken out, so for every one of those sessions the mounting was a guess. The "cornering" G mixed in braking and bumps, and the extra-smooth map line was switched off. The mounting is now found from how the car turns and brakes instead: all four Watkins Glen sessions come out with the same mounting and high confidence, and the smooth line is back with about five times as many points.
  • Cornering G at Watkins Glen still reads low, and that can't be fixed after the fact. The new IMU's built-in processing mistook long, steady cornering for the logger being tilted, and quietly removed part of it. Recorded cornering G is a little over half the real value as the car turns in, and about a third once it's settled into the corner. Braking was mostly unaffected. The values were lost on the sensor itself, so treat Watkins Glen cornering G as a comparison between laps, not a true number.

Changed

  • The logger now records what the new IMU is actually good at. From this version it saves the sensor's raw acceleration, with nothing removed, so cornering G will be the real value. It also saves the IMU's own reading of how the car is pitched and rolled, which was being thrown away. It takes 50 readings a second instead of the 20 it was quietly limited to. This needs a quick bench check on the logger before the next track day.

1.13.0

2 Oct 2026

Changed

  • Each track's dashboard now shows every lap in full detail. It used to thin every lap's map trace, speed and G lines down to about three points a second to keep the page small, so comparing laps across sessions there looked coarser than the same laps on their own session pages. It now carries everything that was recorded, the same as a session page. The page is about twice the size (roughly 1.4 MB to download for Watkins Glen instead of 0.8 MB), and with every lap ticked at once it responds a little more slowly.

1.12.0

2 Oct 2026

Added

  • Dashboards work without an internet connection. The map and chart software now comes with the pages instead of being fetched from the internet each time, so a phone on the paddock hotspot (or anywhere with no signal) can still open them. If that copy is ever missing, the page quietly gets it from the internet instead.

Fixed

  • Pages show up right away on a slow connection. A first visit on a slow phone connection used to show a blank screen for well over ten seconds while the map and chart software downloaded. The page now draws straight away with a "Loading telemetry…" notice, and that download is about half the size it was.
  • A page that can't load tells you so. If the map software can't be downloaded, you get a message and a Reload button instead of an empty page. If only the charts can't be downloaded, the map, lap list and tables still work and a notice says the charts are missing. A download that stalls is retried from the internet after 15 seconds instead of hanging forever.

1.11.2

2 Oct 2026

Fixed

  • The Sector timing board shows up on phones again. On a phone or touch tablet, the Stats tab squeezed it to nothing, so it was simply missing, along with the only way to pick which lap the Sector map shows.
  • Green and purple mean one thing each. Green is always "best of the laps you've selected" and purple is always "fastest of any lap". The Stats and "Where time is lost" tables used to highlight the best of your selection in purple, right under a Sector timing board where purple means something else. Small losses in "Where time is lost" are now yellow, as its help text always said, not green.
  • The track page's header makes sense. It no longer shows a date of 12/31/1969 or an internal session code, and its best lap, theoretical best and consistency now say "(all sessions)", because that's what they cover. A single session's page still says "(session)".
  • Narrow screens no longer scroll sideways. On a small phone, opening the lap list or a long "Best lap" label pushed the page wider than the screen. The All/None buttons now drop under the session name instead.
  • A window exactly 700 pixels wide (a large window at 200% zoom, for instance) no longer opens with the map squeezed to nothing.
  • After going Back to a dashboard, the ticked laps always match the laps drawn. Before, a lap could show as ticked without being plotted.
  • The Laps, Options, Replay and Details headers work from the keyboard. Tab to one and press Enter or Space to open or close it, and screen readers announce whether it's open.

Changed

  • The map toolbar is shorter. Trace buttons with nothing behind them on that page (Corrected and Map-matched, usually) are hidden instead of greyed out. On a computer, the "Options" label above the toolbar is gone, since the toolbar is always open there and clicking it did nothing.
  • The home page's dashboard links name their track ("Watkins Glen International dashboard →") instead of each saying "Track dashboard →".

1.11.1

29 Sep 2026

Fixed

  • The home page's lap counts and best laps now stay up to date. Each session's row was written once, the first time it was published, and never refreshed, so a session that was later re-timed or had laps set aside still showed its old count and best lap. The Watkins Glen rows are now correct, and the 09-26 session shows no best lap, since both its laps lost too much GPS to count.

1.11.0

29 Sep 2026

Changed

  • "Where time is lost" now measures against the laps you have selected, not the best any lap has ever done. A lap with bad data (GPS dropping out, for instance) can post corner times that are impossible, and they used to become the target for every other lap, so you'd be chasing time that was never really there. Untick a lap you don't trust and it no longer counts.
  • Laps where the GPS kept dropping out are now opt-in. If a lap spent 3 seconds or more without a GPS fix (in gaps of 1.5 s or longer), its corner and sector times are guesses across the holes and can be impossibly fast. Those laps are now treated like a flagged lap: struck through in the lap picker with a ⚠, not selected by default, and never used as a best time, the reference lap, a purple sector or a session's best lap on the home page. You can still tick one to compare it; hover it to see how much GPS it lost. At Watkins Glen this catches both laps of the 09-26 session and the last three laps of 09-25 S3; every other lap had no gaps that long at all.

1.10.2

29 Sep 2026

Fixed

  • Corner names no longer sit on top of the distance numbers. They now go in their own row below the tick labels, and names that would run into each other (e.g. Heel (a)/(b)) drop into a second or third row. The chart grows a little at the bottom to fit them.
  • The G charts are smooth instead of jagged. The accelerometer reads in bursts at ~27 Hz and picks up engine and road vibration; thinning that for the page kept a random vibration sample every third of a second, which drew as a spiky, low-resolution line. G is now low-pass filtered (3 Hz, zero-phase, so peaks stay where they happened) before thinning, and the G trace is kept at 10 samples a second or more even on the track page.

1.10.1

28 Sep 2026

Fixed

  • The Delta chart now shows the real gap between laps. It used to measure "how far into the lap" by adding up the gaps between GPS positions. Each position wobbles by about a metre, which put a regular ripple into the line, and every lap's GPS path comes out a slightly different length, so the line slowly slid away from the truth. On one Watkins Glen pair it was almost half a second off at one corner and ended the lap with the wrong lap ahead. Distance now comes from the car's GPS speed, and every lap is lined up with the reference lap at each corner and sector gate and at the finish. The line is smooth, and at every gate and at the finish it now matches the times in the tables exactly. The stacked comparison's delta panel and the Gap column get the same fix.

1.10.0

28 Sep 2026

Fixed

  • The home page's "What's new" link now shows the current version. It kept whatever version number the site first launched with, because each publish only updated the list of tracks and sessions, never the header above it. The header is now refreshed on every publish.

Added

  • F1-style sector timing. A new Sector timing table at the top of Stats shows each selected lap's time in every sector, with a row of small bars under each sector for its mini-sectors: every corner and every straight inside it. The colours work like F1 timing. Purple means the fastest any lap has been there, green the fastest of the laps you have selected, and yellow slower. The same colours apply to the lap time. Hover a bar for its name and time.
  • Sector map. A new Sector map button on the map draws one lap's line in those colours, mini-sector by mini-sector, with the end of each sector marked. Hover a stretch for its name and time. Click a lap in the Sector timing table to choose which lap is drawn; it starts on the fastest selected lap.

1.9.0

28 Sep 2026

Added

  • Corner and sector gates can now be worked out from your laps. Run racetelemetry gates TRACK once you have a few clean laps of a track. It finds each corner from how hard the car was turning, starts it just before the earliest braking and ends it after track-out, puts the sector splits in the middle of straights, and prints the block to paste into config.toml. It keeps the names of corners you've already set up, and shows how many laps were already braking when they crossed each of your current corner starts, so you can see which ones start too late.

Changed

  • Watkins Glen laps are now timed at the real timing loop. The finish line used to be an estimate worked out from the recorded laps. It now sits on the timing loop itself, measured wall to wall, and is extended a little past each wall so a lap whose GPS drifted off the edge still counts. Existing Glen lap times move by a few hundredths of a second at most once they're re-timed.
  • The Stats tables now say what their numbers mean. Every section (Lap, Sectors, Corners, Segment times, G Stats, and "Where time is lost") has an (i). Hover over it on a computer, or tap it on a phone, for a plain-language explanation. Rows are named for what they measure: "Sector 2, gate S1 → gate S2" instead of "S2", "min speed, km/h" beside each corner, "straight" or "corner" beside each segment, and "Peak cornering G" instead of "Peak Lat G". A line above the table explains the highlighting and the small numbers under each value.
  • Watkins Glen's corners are now grouped the way a driver talks about the track, and placed from the laps themselves. Instead of T1 to T12 the Glen has named corners: The 90, the Esses, the Bus Stop and Outer Loop, the Chute, the Toe, the Heel, and Turns 10 and 11. Each corner now starts just before the earliest point anyone braked for it and ends once the car has finished tracking out, so all of a corner's braking counts toward that corner instead of spilling into the straight before it. The Heel and Turns 10 and 11 are each split in two, marked (a) and (b), because the car brakes separately for each half. Both sector splits now sit in the middle of a straight. Copy the [tracks.watkins_glen] block from config.example.toml to pick it up.

Fixed

  • Sessions recorded on the phone Base Station could sit there for weeks without ever reaching a desktop over Syncthing — the phone's own app storage isn't visible to a separate app like Syncthing, so nothing was ever actually landing in the shared folder it watches. The base station loop now mirrors session files into that folder automatically every cycle, removing one that was archived/purged locally but never touching a file it didn't put there itself, however it compares to what's local.

1.8.0

28 Sep 2026

Added

  • Pop the map out into its own window. A new Pop out map button beside the Map heading puts the map in a small window that stays on screen while you scroll. You can watch it while pointing at the stacked comparison even on a screen too short to show both. Drag the window's top bar to move it, drag its bottom-right corner to resize it, and press Dock to put the map back. While it's popped out, the map re-centres on the REF lap's dot whenever the dot gets near the edge, so you can see the track ahead of it. Your browser remembers where you left the window and how big it was. This is available on computers only; on a phone the map already has its own tab.

1.7.0

28 Sep 2026

Added

  • Every selected lap now gets its own dot on the map while you point at the stacked comparison. The fastest selected lap (REF) sits exactly where you're pointing. Each other lap's dot shows where that lap was at the same moment into the lap, so the space between the dots is the time gap, the same way Replay's ghost cars work.
  • Pin a spot on the stacked comparison. Click the chart and the cursor, the table and the map dots stay put, so you can scroll to the map (or study it) without losing your place. The cursor line turns blue while pinned. Use the left and right arrow keys to nudge it 5 m (hold Shift for 50 m), and click the chart again or press Esc to let go.

Fixed

  • The map dot no longer jumps around. It used to snap to the nearest raw GPS reading rather than following the line drawn on the map. At Watkins Glen, where the raw GPS has occasional glitches, that could throw it hundreds of metres off the track. It now glides along the drawn line, and the dots from the Speed and Delta charts do too.

1.6.0

28 Sep 2026

Changed

  • On a phone or tablet, dragging a finger along a chart now reads it instead of zooming. It used to start drawing a zoom box. Now it moves the cursor line and shows every lap's values at that point, the same as moving the mouse on a computer. It works on the Stack, Speed, G-Force and Delta charts. Swiping up or down still scrolls the page. To zoom on a phone, tap a corner in the "Where time is lost" or Stats table, and use the new Reset zoom button to get back to the whole lap.
  • The stacked comparison's table now says what each number means. The columns are Elapsed (time since the lap started), Speed, Gap to the reference lap (red when behind, green when ahead) and Long. G (with "braking", "accel" or "neutral" beside it). The lap everything is compared against is marked REF, and each other lap's speed shows how much faster or slower it is than REF at that point. Hover over a column heading for an explanation, or tap the (i) next to it on a phone.

1.5.0

27 Sep 2026

Fixed

  • njmp_lightning's start/finish coordinate was wrong by about 26 km — a stale placeholder from before anyone had actually measured the real track, still sitting in the example config unnoticed. Corrected, along with a full set of sector (23) and corner (T1-T10) gates for it, so it gets the same lap analysis Watkins Glen already has.
  • The public race-results site's "What's new" page had been frozen since the site was first set up — the automatic publish step never actually copied it, so every real change since then only ever reached the local site, not the public one. It's now copied on every publish.
  • A session removed locally (an archived raw/duplicate copy) could linger on the public site forever, since the automatic publisher only ever added or updated pages, never removed one. It now remembers what it has pushed before and removes a session's page (and its row on the home page) once that session is gone locally — without ever touching a page it didn't push itself, so another machine's sessions stay untouched.

Added

  • The "Where time is lost" table now says why, not just how much. Each losing lap is compared against whichever selected lap is currently fastest, using its longitudinal G trace to find where braking started and where the throttle went back on, and flags the likely cause in plain language: braked earlier and lost entry speed, slower on entry with the same brake point (usually a knock-on from the corner before), lost speed at the apex, or back to power later on exit. Needs longitudinal-G (IMU) data for both laps being compared; a corner too close in outright pace to say anything meaningful about is left blank rather than guessing.
  • A "Where time is lost" table, ranking corners by how much time was lost there versus the best any lap has ever managed (not just the laps you currently have picked). Click a corner to zoom the stacked comparison view straight to it — the map pans there too, and on a phone it switches to the Stack tab automatically. The existing Corners and Segment-times rows in the main stats table are clickable the same way.
  • A stacked comparison view: speed, delta and longitudinal G on one screen with a shared cursor. The three charts are stacked on a single distance axis (zoom or pan one and they all follow), under the segment strip. One cursor line runs through all three as you hover or touch, the map shows a dot at that point on the track, and a table beneath lists every selected lap's time, speed, gap to the fastest lap and longitudinal G at that distance, along with which corner or straight you're in. On a phone it's a new "Stack" tab; on a wide screen it's a panel right under the map. It's the quickest way to see, say, where in the bus stop one lap gets slower and what the car was doing there.
  • A segment strip above the speed, G and delta charts, and a "Segment times" table. The track is split at your corner gates into straights and corners in lap order (the run to T1, T1, T1→T2, T2, … T12, the run home), drawn as a row of numbered blocks lined up with the chart's distance axis (they move with pan and zoom). With two or more laps picked, each block is the colour of the lap that was quickest through that part of the track. With one lap picked, it's green-to-red by how much slower it was there than the best any lap managed — so "where am I losing time" is visible at a glance. Hover a block for every lap's time in it and the gap to the best. The stats table gains a row per segment with the best highlighted; the times always add up to the lap time. Needs a track with corner gates configured.
  • On a phone, a chart now keeps filling its tab after you tick or untick a lap instead of shrinking to a short strip until you switched tabs.
  • Watkins Glen now has its sectors and corners set up (S1/S2 and T1-T12), along with its start/finish gate line, in the example config. The dashboard's stats table shows sector splits and per-corner entry, minimum and exit speeds for every Glen lap, and the map and charts mark the gates. Copy the [tracks.watkins_glen] block from config.example.toml.
  • The phone Base Station can publish new sessions to the public results site by itself. After each dashboard build it pushes chosen tracks to the GitHub Pages repo, adding to what's already there rather than replacing it, and retries if there's no signal. Only tracks you list are ever pushed, so a test loop near home stays private. See docs/publishing.md.
  • A new racetelemetry derive-outings command lets the Logger run all day without stopping and still feed the Base Station's dashboard pipeline. Previously, leaving record running continuously (rather than stopping it after every run) meant the raw file never looked "finished," so nothing ever reached the shared dashboard until the Logger was powered off at the end of the day. This periodically pulls out each completed run as soon as the car comes to a real stop back in the pits — using genuine sensor stillness, not just a GPS speed reading — so a fresh, ordinary session shows up for the Base Station to grab every time you finish a stint, while the full-day recording keeps going underneath. Nothing is derived while you're still out on track.
  • On a phone, the dashboard's Map/Stats/Speed/G-Force/Delta panels are now separate full-screen tabs instead of one long scrolling page. The map and each chart capture touch/swipe for their own pan/zoom, which used to leave no safe empty margin to scroll past them to reach the next panel. A bottom tab bar switches between them; each tab fills the available screen without needing to scroll (the stats table can still scroll internally if it doesn't fit). The map's toolbar and the header's date/session-stat chips also collapse behind a tap by default now, reclaiming space they used to take up permanently — the header alone used to run to a third of the screen on some sessions. Each tab's own card-style background/border is gone too, since there's only ever one panel visible at a time — nothing left for it to visually separate from. Detects touch devices directly rather than assuming a narrow screen means touch, so a folding phone's larger unfolded screen gets the same treatment instead of falling back to the original layout. Desktop is unchanged — all of this only applies at phone width.
  • Every report page now has a real browser-tab title — e.g. "NJMP – Lightning · 2026-07-11 · S2 (0dc27a17) — Map" — instead of a blank tab or the bare filename. Multiple sessions' pages used to be indistinguishable from each other once open in a browser or a tab switcher; now each names its session and which view it is.
  • The Base Station keeps the shared dashboard site up to date on its own. On the Pi kept with the car kit, three background services now pull new sessions from the Logger, rebuild the dashboards whenever a session lands, and serve the result on the local network — so anyone in the paddock just opens http://<base-station>:8080/ on a phone and sees the latest laps, with nobody running commands. Sessions show up about two minutes after the car comes in. Setup is in the runbook (§ 8, "the Base Station auto-pipeline"). A new racetelemetry serve command does the serving and can also be run by hand.
  • racetelemetry status-server — a phone/laptop-reachable page for checking the Logger's health and editing its settings without SSH. Once the Pi is sealed in the car, this is meant to replace pulling it back out to poke at it: a live status page shows GPS fix age/quality, IMU activity, and Pi health (CPU temp, throttle state, disk space), and a settings page lets you change hardware/GPS/IMU/data-directory/track settings from a form — validated and backed up before anything is saved. Runs independently of record, so it's reachable any time you're on the same network as the Pi (e.g. the track hotspot), including before a session starts.
  • racetelemetry diagnose — a live sensor readout for checking wiring before you trust it with a real session. Opens GPS and IMU directly and shows a continuously-updating terminal readout (fix status, position, speed, and now actual accel/gyro numbers, not just "recently updated") — useful right after wiring up new hardware, or any time you want to confirm a sensor is alive without going through record's wait-for-GPS-fix startup. If only one sensor is connected so far, that sensor's readout still works instead of the whole command refusing to start. It's also visible from status-server's page on a phone or the Base Station's browser while it runs, so you don't need a terminal open on the Pi itself to watch it.
  • status-server's live status page now shows real accelerometer and gyroscope numbers, not just "IMU updated 0.3s ago." Useful for confirming a sensor is actually reporting sane values, not just that something is technically still writing.
  • The IMU can now recover on its own from a frozen sensor stream, and you can see it happening. The BNO085 occasionally wedges into repeating the same stale reading forever with no error — previously this was silently suppressed and invisible from anywhere, including a 22-hour bench run where it froze a few minutes in and never came back on its own. If you've wired the sensor's reset pin (imu.bno085_reset_pin in config.toml — see the wiring doc), the Logger now actively resets and reconfigures the sensor when this happens, and the status page shows an "Auto-recovery attempts" count so you can tell from a phone whether this is occurring, rather than only discovering a session has a silent IMU gap after the fact.
  • The new MAX-M10S GPS module now records at a genuine 10 times per second, not the 1 Hz it was limited to before. This chip needed a different setup sequence than older GPS modules use (gps_backend = "ublox_m10" in config.toml); getting there also raises the module's data rate over the wire to keep up with the faster fix rate. Lap timing and track traces both benefit — the previous 1 Hz rate meant up to a full second between position updates.
  • A back link on published dashboard pages. When browsing a published site (via publish), each session and track dashboard now shows a "&larr; All tracks" chip in the header that takes you back to the site's home page. Only appears on published pages — a standalone dashboard or report run has no home page to link back to, so it stays hidden there.
  • A longitudinal G chart, directly under the speed trace. Same distance axis, so a braking zone can be read against the speed it produced: where you got on the brakes, how hard, how long you held it, and where the power came back in. Negative is braking, positive is drive — unlike the map's G-force colouring, which shows only how hard the car is working and can't tell the two apart. Drawn at the IMU's full rate, so short events survive. The panel hides itself for sessions with no IMU data.
  • CPU temperature and throttle-state logging during recording. The Pi now writes its own health data to the session file every 5 seconds alongside GPS and IMU samples. After a session you can query system_samples to see whether the Pi ever heat-throttled or hit low-voltage during a run — useful early warning if the total-loss electrical system is running lean or the enclosure is getting too warm. Only active on a Raspberry Pi; runs silently as a no-op on a development laptop.
  • racetelemetry sf-line — define start/finish as a line across the track rather than a circle around a point. The circle decides a lap boundary from the midpoint of the time spent inside it, which shifts with speed and line; a gate line works out the exact instant the car crossed. On the real NJMP sessions this cut the lap-to-lap variation in *where along the track* a lap starts from about 3.5 m to about 4 cm. The command derives the gate automatically from a track's reference line (perpendicular to the direction of travel), or --interactive opens a click-to-place map. Follow with retime to re-time sessions you've already recorded. Worth knowing: laps still won't start at exactly the same *point*, because you genuinely cross the line at different places across its width. That part isn't error, and this doesn't change it.
  • The BNO085 IMU now records real gyroscope (yaw/pitch/roll rate) data. Previously it always logged 0.0 for all three gyro axes — the chip's gyroscope report was never turned on, only accelerometer and orientation. A known quirk of this chip's firmware means the very first reading right after the gyroscope turns on can come back as a nonsense value far beyond anything physically possible (a spike of thousands of degrees per second); that one reading is now recognised and dropped rather than logged as real motion.

Changed

  • status-server's live status page no longer flashes and resets your scroll position every 2 seconds. It used to reload the entire page on a timer; now it quietly updates each section (Recording, GPS, IMU, Pi health) in place, a little faster than before too.
  • The Grid-aligned trace is now built from the Smoothed line rather than raw GPS. Its whole purpose is making a genuine line difference legible, and feeding it raw positions carried GPS jitter straight into the comparison — exactly the noise you're trying to see past. Falls back to raw for laps with no smoothed trace.
  • The Smoothed map trace's IMU-rate detail now estimates heading once per session instead of once per lap, reusing that one pass for every lap rather than paying for it again each time. Dashboards build faster on multi-lap sessions, and heading tends to come out slightly more accurate too — it settles in better with a genuine stationary stretch to anchor it, which the whole session is far more likely to include than any one lap.

Fixed

  • A genuine stop could show the car's position slowly sliding across the road on the map, even though it wasn't moving. That was the Raw GPS trace showing real GPS noise — expected, and exactly what the Smoothed trace's stop-detection already corrects for — but Raw was the map's default view, not Smoothed. The map now defaults to Smoothed.
  • The Smoothed trace's stop-correction only patched the stop itself, not the real position error in the moving trace leading up to and away from it — a first attempt at smoothing the seam between them was cosmetic and didn't touch that. The fix instead tells the fusion filter directly that the car is essentially motionless during a detected stop, so its own smoothing pass (which already looks both forward and backward in time) reconciles that certainty with the approach and departure, correcting the underlying estimate rather than papering over the transition.
  • A confirmed stop (GPS speed reads essentially zero) could still show a couple of metres of smooth, wandering drift on the Smoothed trace — investigated against a real ~90s stop and confirmed this is genuine GPS receiver noise even at good signal quality, not a filter bug (the noise model's own predicted accuracy for that signal quality was never exceeded) — it's just large enough, in absolute terms, to visibly cross a narrow lane. The map now holds a confirmed stop to one steady position (blended smoothly in and out, not a snap) instead of continuing to re-draw every sample of that real, but expected, wander.
  • On a phone, the map's Replay controls could end up hidden underneath the bottom tab bar. The Map tab's height didn't account for a strip of page padding above it, so it ran that much past the tab bar and its last row — the Replay header — sat behind it. The tab now fits exactly, and an expanded Replay panel scrolls internally instead of crowding the map. The phone layout also drops the leftover page margin around each tab, so the map and charts use the full screen width and the Replay row has room to breathe above the tab bar.
  • On a phone or unfolded foldable, the dashboard header wastes less space. The best-lap chip and Details toggle now sit beside the title when there's room for them, instead of always dropping onto their own line.
  • Session dashboards opened from the Base Station now have a way back to the session list. A "← All sessions" chip appears in the header whenever the list is available, instead of you having to edit the address bar. (Published-site pages already had "← All tracks".)
  • The Base Station's automatic pull no longer breaks each time the phone's hotspot restarts. The hotspot hands out a different network address on every restart, and the pull only trusted the Logger at addresses it had already seen, so it was silently refused ("Host key verification failed") and no sessions arrived. It now recognises the Logger by its own identity instead of its address (and still refuses if that identity ever changes).
  • The Logger's GPS now recovers by itself if the GPS module resets in the middle of a run. On a real practice day the module reset mid-session, dropping back to its factory settings, and the Logger kept listening as if nothing had changed — so GPS silently stopped recording partway through, with the IMU logging on for another ten minutes of driving and no error anywhere. If no valid GPS data arrives for five seconds, the Logger now works out what state the module is in, sets it up again, and carries on, logging what it did. The status page shows an "Auto-recovery attempts" count for GPS (as it already does for the IMU). This restores recording after a few seconds' gap; it doesn't stop the module resetting in the first place — see the wiring notes.
  • The GPS module now sends only the two messages the Logger uses, freeing up its serial link. The command to switch off the unused ones had two wrong identifiers, so it was rejected every time (a warning in the logs on every start) and the module kept flooding the line with ~5x the data needed. Fixed and checked on the real module; the settings are also saved in the module's battery-backed memory, so a module reset comes back already configured. (This board has no configuration flash, so that's the most it can keep.)
  • The Logger now sets its own clock from GPS at boot, so sessions get the right date even with no network. The Pi has no built-in clock: started away from a hotspot it resumed from the last time it was shut down, so a whole practice session was dated nine days early, and its sensor timestamps then jumped when the Pi later joined a hotspot and corrected itself mid-recording. A small step before recording starts now reads the first GPS fix's time and sets the clock, then hands the GPS back.
  • Lap traces now start and end exactly on the start/finish line. Each lap used to be cut at the nearest GPS samples, up to ~5 m either side of the line at speed, so no two laps began or ended the same distance from it and the distance-based charts (speed, delta) never quite lined up; a trace also finished slightly before its own lap time. Laps are now cut at the exact crossing point.
  • Lap times at Watkins Glen are now timed with a proper start/finish gate line instead of a circle. With just a point, a crossing was timed from when the car entered and left a 30 m circle, which drifted by up to ~0.15 s (and by nearly half a second when the car took a wide line, or when GPS dropped out right on the line — one lap was 0.48 s too long and the next 0.43 s too short). Today's sessions were re-timed; new recordings use the gate automatically.
  • A GPS outage mid-lap is now shown as one, instead of a straight line that looks like driven track. When the GPS lost the car for a couple of seconds or more (a real lap lost ~7 s over its last corner), the map joined the two fixes with a straight segment and the speed chart with a smooth line, both of which looked like real data. The map now draws each gap as a thin dashed line labelled with how long and roughly how far went unmeasured, the speed chart breaks the line, and a lap that lost 3 s or more of GPS carries a ⚠ in the lap list with the details on hover. The lap time itself is unaffected when the start/finish crossing was timed cleanly.
  • The map's colour-by-speed and colour-by-G-force modes could look noticeably blurrier than the plain line for the same lap. Both capped every lap at about 150 coloured segments no matter how many GPS points were actually recorded, discarding most of a normal lap's real detail. A typical lap now renders every point — the same resolution the plain line already gets — with a much higher ceiling kept only for genuinely dense traces.
  • A real drive could log a handful of impossible G-force spikes right when the IMU's auto-recovery kicked in. A brief hiccup in the sensor (already self-healing, see above) could momentarily report a wildly impossible single-axis reading a few times in a row — short enough that it wasn't caught by the existing frozen-sensor safety net. It's now recognized and dropped the same way an earlier, similar gyroscope glitch already was. Found on a real ~55-minute test drive, affecting 0.15% of IMU samples in that one session (already-recorded data isn't retroactively edited, so that specific session's G-force chart may still show brief spikes at the two moments this happened).
  • Saving settings from status-server's settings page could fail with a permission error on some Base Station setups (e.g. an Android/Termux device) because the save-check used a fixed system folder that isn't always writable there. It now uses whatever temp folder the system actually provides.
  • status-server's GPS panel could show the literal text "None, None" for position and "None km/h" for speed before a GPS fix ever arrived, instead of a plain "—". Only visible before this release in a narrow window right as record started; the new diagnose mode can sit in a real no-fix state for a while, which is what surfaced it.
  • Stopping record (or diagnose) with Ctrl-C could leave status-server showing "recording"/"diagnostic mode" for up to 10 seconds after the process had already exited cleanly, because the live-status heartbeat's very last write could race ahead of the shutdown flag that marks it stopped.
  • The BNO085 could silently freeze on the same accel/gyro reading forever after a while, with no error and no indication anything was wrong — found using diagnose's new live readout. Root cause was two-fold: a Raspberry Pi I2C clock-stretching bug this sensor is especially prone to triggering at the Pi's default bus speed (see docs/runbook.md's troubleshooting table for the fix — slowing the I2C bus down), and the driver having no way to tell "real sensor data" apart from "the same cached reading served over and over." It now recognises dozens of bit-for-bit identical readings in a row as a stuck sensor rather than real data, and reports it as no new data arriving — safe for the perfectly normal case of the Logger sitting completely still for a long time, since real sensor noise means a live sensor's readings never stay *exactly* identical for that long.
  • The map's G-force colour mode could mis-colour a lap's line on a thinned-down view (any --resolution other than full) when the G-force and position traces happened to end up with the same number of plotted points after thinning — a coincidence, not a sign they line up in time.
  • Replay's Play/scrub could stop just short of a lap's actual last point when viewing the Smoothed trace on a lap with good IMU coverage — that trace can run a little denser (and later) than the raw GPS points the scrub bar used to measure its length against. It now runs the full length of whichever trace is on screen.
  • sf-line's derived gate could come out at a wrong angle for a track whose saved reference line isn't a genuine closed loop. Closed-circuit tracks were never affected; only a point-to-point or oval-shaped one.
  • The pick-sectors/pick-corners/sf-line --interactive pages could render with broken layout if a track's name in config.toml contained characters like &, <, or ".
  • Recording could print an alarming-looking error right at Ctrl-C, in a narrow timing window, even though the session had already saved cleanly. Harmless either way, but a clean stop now looks clean.
  • Fixed the dashboard's header bar sometimes appearing behind the map when scrolling. The header now always stays on top.

1.4.0

25 Jul 2026

Added

  • The Smoothed map trace now has roughly 10x more detail between GPS points. It used to draw one point per GPS fix — about ten a second — with everything in between left as a straight line. It now follows the car's actual measured motion between fixes, so a quick direction change or a corner turn-in shows up as real curvature instead of a chord. Needs IMU data and a confident calibration for the session; laps without them look exactly as before. Pages get noticeably bigger at full detail — --resolution auto keeps them to a sensible size automatically.
  • A "Grid-aligned" map trace, for telling a real line difference from a sampling artefact. GPS points land wherever they happen to fall, so two laps driving the *same* line through a corner can still draw visibly different shapes — which makes "did I actually take a different line?" genuinely hard to answer, especially comparing laps from different sessions. This trace puts every lap's points at the same places around the track, so anything you see is a real difference. It's still each lap's own position — nothing is snapped to a reference line — and unlike Map-matched it works on any session, with no setup. Colour modes show solid on it.
  • You can now choose how much detail a dashboard page carries. racetelemetry dashboard --resolution takes full (every recorded sample, and still the default for a single session), auto (thin only as far as needed to keep the page a sensible size, with --budget-mb), or a rate in Hz like --resolution 5. Track dashboards keep their existing lighter default, since they aggregate many sessions. Lap times, sector splits and corner stats are always computed from the full recorded data first — this only changes what travels to the page.

Changed

  • G-force detail on the map is roughly 9x finer. The G trace was being averaged down to match the 10-per-second GPS points before it reached the page, throwing away about nine of every ten readings the sensor actually recorded. It now travels at full recorded rate, so quick events — kerb strikes, a suspension hit, the initial bite of the brakes — show up in the map's G-force colouring instead of being smoothed away. Pages get bigger (about 1.9x on a typical session); use --resolution if you want them lighter.

Fixed

  • Map traces no longer look jagged on smooth parts of the track. Positions were being written to the page rounded to about a metre, which was invisible when traces had a point every few metres but turned the new, much denser traces into a visible staircase — worst on straights that don't happen to run north-south or east-west. Positions now carry enough decimal places (about a centimetre) that the rounding can't be seen. This also slightly cleans up the raw and grid-aligned traces, which were being affected by the same thing.
  • The km/h ↔ mph toggle now updates the lap replay table too. Its Speed and "Δ vs ref" columns kept the previous units until replay was played or scrubbed.
  • --resolution auto no longer silently ignores its size budget. It could pick a target that rounded to "change nothing", leaving a page well over the requested size. It now checks that a setting genuinely fits before using it.

1.3.0

24 Jul 2026

Changed

  • The Smoothed map trace (show --fuse and the dashboard's Smoothed toggle) is now more detailed between GPS points, when a session has IMU data and a usable calibration. It used to only assume steady speed between each 10Hz GPS fix; it now uses the car's actual measured motion (from the IMU) to fill in that gap, so quick changes in speed or direction show up between fixes instead of being smoothed into a straight line. Sessions without IMU coverage, or where the calibration isn't confident enough, look exactly as before.

1.2.0

21 Jul 2026

Added

  • The Map-matched trace is now smoothed. It already snapped a lap's position onto the saved reference line; that reference line itself can be slightly jagged point-to-point (it's built from a per-point median across many laps), which used to show up as visible zig-zag as a lap moved along it. The line drawn is now a lightly smoothed version — how far along the lap the car is judged to be, and the off-line distance shown elsewhere, are unaffected.

1.1.0

20 Jul 2026

Added

  • A "Map-matched" map trace option, alongside Raw/Smoothed/Corrected — snaps a lap's position to the saved reference line using how far the car has travelled (not its raw, possibly-biased GPS position) to figure out where along the track it actually is. For a session with severe, sustained GPS bias — smoothly wrong for tens of seconds at a time, not just brief jitter — this is the only trace that stays usable, at the cost of throwing away any real within-lap line variation for the stretches it engages on. Meant for comparing lap *progress* (where was I faster or slower) on an otherwise-untrustworthy session, not for line analysis. Disabled until a reference line has been built for the track (racetelemetry trackline).

Fixed

  • A single bad GPS reading can no longer teleport a lap's map trace. Occasionally a GPS fix reports itself as perfectly good (fine signal quality) while its position is simply wrong by hundreds of metres — usually right after a brief signal interruption. That fix is now recognised as impossible (no racecar covers that distance that fast) and ignored, the same way a genuinely missing fix already was, instead of being trusted and dragging the smoothed trace, the reference line, and the "off-line" check along with it.

1.0.0

20 Jul 2026

Added

  • The dashboard — one page per session with a map, a lap-by-lap stats table, speed and "delta vs. reference lap" charts, and sector/corner splits, replacing a pile of separate map/chart files you used to have to flip between.
  • Track dashboards — a second dashboard that combines every session ever recorded at a given track into one page, so you can compare a lap from today against a lap from months ago, not just laps within one outing.
  • Lap replay — play back one or several laps at once on the map with a scrub bar and 1x/2x/4x speed control: a ghost marker per lap moves along the track in sync, with a live table of each lap's speed and its gap to the reference lap at that same moment.
  • A published, shareable site (racetelemetry publish) — builds a folder of dashboards you can put on GitHub Pages so a co-driver, parent, or teammate can browse results themselves, without you emailing files around.
  • Automatic track recognition — the recorder figures out which track you're at from the first GPS fix of the session; no need to tell it by hand.
  • GPS quality tools — a smoothing filter that cuts GPS jitter, a "reference line" built from the median of every lap ever recorded at a track, a tool to flag laps that drifted off that line (or off the track entirely), and a slow-drift correction tool for GPS that wanders over the course of a session.
  • G-force colouring on the map — colour a lap's trace by how hard the car is working (combined lateral + braking/accel G), the same colour scale as the standalone gg-map view.
  • In-dashboard gate editing — drag sector and corner markers directly on the map to fine-tune where split times are measured, with a one-click export you (or the owner) can paste into the track's configuration to make it permanent for everyone.
  • A km/h ↔ mph toggle for anyone more comfortable reading speeds in imperial units.
  • A mobile-friendly layout — the lap list and replay controls collapse down to a single line on a phone screen instead of crowding it.

Changed

  • Lap timing accuracy — a lap's start/finish crossing is now stamped at the midpoint of the car's pass through the finish area rather than the moment it exits, which used to drift with how fast the car was going. Where a precise finish *line* (rather than just a circle) has been set up for a track, timing is tighter still — accurate to roughly ±10-20 milliseconds instead of ±100ms+. Sessions recorded before this change can be re-timed after the fact without re-recording.
  • The dashboard's delta chart (how far ahead/behind you are through the lap) now compares against whichever lap is fastest among the ones you've currently ticked on, rather than always the single fastest lap of the whole session.

Fixed

  • A single bad GPS reading could previously stretch a lap's whole distance scale, leaving sector and corner markers stranded well past where that lap's own speed trace actually ends on the chart. Distance calculations now discard those physically-impossible jumps.
  • A session with drift-corrected GPS data used to permanently greyed-out the "Smoothed" trace option on the map, even though it had already been computed. Both the smoothed and corrected traces are now kept and offered as separate choices.
